VISION
1. Product Name
Working name: Verified Proof-of-Work Developer Marketplace
Primary positioning:
Find software like what you want. Hire the people who built it.
Developer positioning:
Build. Show. Get hired.
2. Purpose
Build a global marketplace where developers showcase real software products, demos, deployments, and client-safe case studies; clients discover relevant builders through proof-of-work; and qualified project inquiries are routed to individual developers or, later, teams/studios.
The product is not a generic social network and is not an Upwork-style bidding marketplace.
The V1 business loop is:
Developer publishes proof
→ Client discovers a relevant product
→ Client watches/opens the demo
→ Client submits "Build Something Similar"
→ Developer responds
→ External discussion / proposal / contract
→ Client confirms hire
→ Project completes
→ Verified review
→ More trust and discovery
3. Product Principles
Proof before pitch — working products and verified contribution matter more than self-written skill claims.
Business-problem-first discovery — clients search for CRM, SaaS, e-commerce, booking, inventory, AI automation, dashboards, etc.; technology filters are secondary.
Low client friction — browsing is public; inquiry is lightweight; account creation is not forced before a client sees value.
Trust is earned, not bought — verification, reviews, response reliability, and marketplace outcomes cannot be directly purchased.
Quality beats volume — curated builders and strong showcases are more important than registration count.
No bidding race-to-the-bottom — clients discover evidence and choose builders; the product avoids mass proposal spam.
External contracts/payments in V1 — the platform proves lead generation first.
Global destination, curated launch — any geography and software category may eventually participate, but initial content is manually approved.
Monetize value, not access — clients browse and inquire freely; paid tiers later sell business tools, capacity, analytics, recruiting, and workflow.
Build only what validates the marketplace — teams, payments, source-code sales, AI matching, and hosted demos come later.

6. Showcase Slot Model
Every newly approved developer receives:
3 active showcase slots
Unlimited drafts
Unlimited archived showcases
Archiving frees a slot
A successful showcase is never consumed by a hire
Later, verified marketplace success can unlock more capacity
Later, Pro plans can increase active capacity
This mechanism exists to:
Reduce project spam
Improve average showcase quality
Encourage developers to keep portfolios current
Create a fair free tier
Preserve future subscription monetization

9. Success Criteria
9.1 V1 Validation Targets
20 manually curated developers
50–60 approved high-quality showcases
Majority of showcases include a live demo, demo video, or credible client-safe case study
5–10 qualified client inquiries
2–5 serious developer/client discussions
1–3 paid engagements originating from the platform
Inquiry → response tracking enabled
Hire/completion outcome capture enabled
9.2 North-Star Metric
V1: Qualified Client–Builder Connections per Week
Later:
Successful Client–Builder Hires per Month
Completed Marketplace GMV
Repeat/maintenance project volume
10. Anti-Goals
The product must not become:
A generic LinkedIn-style posting network
A follower/like popularity contest
A cheap-freelancer bidding site
A dumping ground for tutorial clones
A pay-to-win search directory
A source-code marketplace before verification infrastructure exists
A distributed-systems architecture showcase before product-market fit

12. Final Product Rule
The platform is successful only when both are repeatedly true:
Developer: "I got paid work because I showed my work here."
Client: "I found the right builder faster because I could see what they had already built."
